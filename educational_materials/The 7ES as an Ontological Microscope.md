## 3. Applying the Lenses by Functional Role

### 3.1 The Citizen (Understanding Local City Council)

**Best lens:** Calculus Edition (7 elements at the coarsest level) + Structural lens (Modal Extension's Structural mode)

| Element | What the Citizen Sees |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Input** | Tax revenue, citizen complaints, federal mandates, public hearings |
| **Output** | Services (roads, schools, police), regulations, permits, decisions |
| **Processing** | City council meetings, committee deliberations, staff analysis |
| **Controls** | City charter, state law, budget constraints, election cycles |
| **Feedback** | Election results, public opinion, media coverage, service complaints |
| **Interface** | City-citizen interactions, public comment periods, town halls |
| **Environment** | State government, federal policy, economic conditions, demographic shifts |

**Why this works:** The citizen doesn't need to know about energetic vs. informational input modes—they need to see *who is doing what, to whom, and with what effect*. The Calculus Edition provides that clarity.

**Question to ask:** "Which element is broken?" → If input is ignored, the city doesn't respond to citizen needs. If feedback is suppressed, the city doesn't learn. If controls are too rigid, the city can't adapt.

### 3.2 The Policy Analyst (Designing Interventions)

**Best lens:** 28-Mode Edition (E, I, R, T) + Modal Extension's Dynamic/Structural distinction

| Element | Policy Analyst's Question |
|---------|---------------------------|
| **Input** | Are we receiving *energetic* resources (funding, staff) and *informational* resources (data, expertise) in adequate amounts? |
| **Processing** | Is our *dynamic* processing (active analysis, deliberation) balanced with *structural* processing (institutional memory, established procedures)? |
| **Controls** | Are our *enforced* controls (regulations, oversight) appropriate, or are *inherent* controls (institutional culture, norms) doing the heavy lifting? |
| **Feedback** | Do we have *active* feedback (performance metrics, evaluations) AND *passive* feedback (continued operation, public trust) confirming viability? |

**Why this works:** Policy analysts need to design interventions that increase variety (Ashby compliance). The 28-mode framework tells them *what kind* of variety is needed—energetic, informational, relational, or temporal. The Modal Extension tells them *how* that variety should be delivered—actively (via new programs) or structurally (via institutional redesign).

### 3.3 The Business Analyst (Optimizing Operations)

**Best lens:** 28-Mode Edition (focus on Energetic and Informational modes) + Modal Extension's Dynamic/Structural distinction

| Element | Business Analyst's Question |
|---------|----------------------------|
| **Input** | Are we acquiring resources (E) and information (I) efficiently? Are we missing *relational* inputs (partnerships, market position) or *temporal* inputs (timing, cycles)? |
| **Processing** | Is our *dynamic* processing (active decision-making, innovation) properly coupled with *structural* processing (established workflows, best practices)? |
| **Output** | Are we producing the right *energetic* outputs (products, services) and *informational* outputs (reports, communications)? Are we ignoring *relational* outputs (brand value, network effects)? |
| **Feedback** | Do we have *active* feedback (KPIs, customer surveys) AND *passive* feedback (continued market presence, employee retention) confirming viability? |

**Why this works:** Business is fundamentally about *flows*—of money, materials, information, and value. The 28-Mode framework maps these flows precisely. The Modal Extension distinguishes between *active* flows (deliberate, costly) and *structural* flows (ambient, persistent), which is crucial for cost-benefit analysis.
