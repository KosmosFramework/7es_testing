## Part I: System Identification and Boundary Definition

The "Cosmic Horizon" is not a monolithic object. A cosmological horizon is one of two boundaries in three-dimensional space: the particle horizon or the event horizon. The particle horizon divides space into those points reached by light traveling to an observer from those points too distant for light to have been observed. The event horizon includes all possible future observations. Additionally, the Hubble radius defines the boundary of causal processes as it defines the distance which light can travel during one Hubble time.

For the purposes of this 7ES analysis, the system S is defined as the composite cosmological horizon structure as experienced by a comoving observer, encompassing the particle horizon, the cosmological event horizon, and the Hubble sphere. The system boundary is the horizon surface itself. The "interior" is the observable universe accessible to the observer; the "exterior" is the causally disconnected region beyond.

This composite identification is itself a significant finding: the cosmic horizon is a multi-subsystem structure at the most fundamental level of definition, with each horizon type representing a different causal constraint operating through a different mechanism.
