### Element 1: INPUT (I) — How Dark Matter Acquires Resources

Dark matter input exhibits at least three distinct subsystems operating through different mechanisms.

**I_E (Energetic Input):** Dark matter acquires mass-energy through multiple pathways. The primary channel is gravitational accretion: matter falls into gravitational potential wells established by primordial density perturbations. In the hierarchical bottom-up scenario, low mass halos form earlier, while massive ones are assembled at later times through mergers of smaller mass halos and accretion of dark matter particles from the surrounding density field. A second channel is merger events, in which entire halos combine. The assembly of virialized objects in Cold Dark Matter cosmologies involves countless merger and accretion events that can convulse the interiors of growing halos. A possible third channel, relevant to SIDM models, involves energy redistribution through self-interaction collisions. This element operates through multiple parallel subsystems — smooth accretion, discrete mergers, and (potentially) collisional energy exchange — each with distinct dynamics.

**I_I (Informational Input):** This is the first major mode deficiency. Dark matter receives essentially no electromagnetic information from its environment. It does not absorb photons. It does not couple to the electromagnetic field. Dark matter appears not to interact via the electromagnetic force, and therefore neither emits nor reflects light. The only informational input channel is gravitational: the distribution of mass in the environment encodes positional and dynamical information through the gravitational potential. This is an extremely low-bandwidth informational input compared to baryonic systems.

**I_R (Relational Input):** Dark matter receives relational context through spacetime geometry. The boundary conditions of the cosmological density field, set by primordial quantum fluctuations amplified during inflation, provide the initial relational context. Dark matter fluctuations could grow denser and more massive even before the release of the CMB, whereas the growth of ordinary matter fluctuations was inhibited by radiation. The relational input is rich but operates exclusively through the gravitational channel.

**I_T (Temporal Input):** Temporal input includes the cosmological epoch (expansion rate H(t)), the age of the universe at any given moment, and the timescale of structure formation. New research shows that dark matter particles could have been moving near the speed of light shortly after the Big Bang, only to cool down later and still help form galaxies. This finding is significant — it indicates that the temporal input (cosmological epoch) fundamentally changes the character of the system, from "hot" early behavior to "cold" late-time behavior. This is a temporal input driving a processing-mode transition.

**Input Subsystem Assessment:** Three distinct input pathways identified (smooth accretion, merger, possible self-interaction). I_I mode is profoundly deficient — only gravitational channel operational. This is a *structural* deficiency, not a contingent one.

### Element 2: OUTPUT (O) — What Dark Matter Produces

**O_E (Energetic Output):** Dark matter outputs energy through gravitational effects. It performs gravitational work on baryonic matter, deepening potential wells that drive gas infall and eventually star formation. It also outputs energy through dynamical friction — when a massive body moves through a dark matter halo, it loses kinetic energy to the halo particles. In SIDM scenarios, the self-interactions allow energy and momentum transport through halos, altering their structure and dynamics relative to those produced by collisionless dark matter.

**O_I (Informational Output):** This is the second critical mode deficiency. Dark matter produces almost no direct informational output. It emits no photons, no neutrinos (in standard CDM), no detectable particles. Its only informational output is indirect: the gravitational field it generates, which can be "read" by baryonic instruments through gravitational lensing, rotation curve measurements, and dynamical mass estimates. The curvature of space-time near any gravitating mass (including dark matter) deflects passing rays of light — observably shifting, distorting and magnifying the images of background galaxies.

**O_R (Relational Output):** This is where dark matter's output is most powerful. Dark matter creates the relational scaffolding of the universe. Dark matter is thought to serve as gravitational scaffolding for cosmic structures. After the Big Bang, dark matter clumped into blobs along narrow filaments with superclusters of galaxies forming a cosmic web. It establishes gravitational potential wells, defines the geometry of cosmic structure, and determines the topology of the cosmic web. This is a massive relational output.

**O_T (Temporal Output):** Dark matter establishes long-duration temporal structures. Dark matter halos, once formed, persist for billions of years. They set the timescale for galaxy formation and evolution. The orbital periods of matter within halos create rhythmic temporal structures.

**Output Subsystem Assessment:** O_R and O_T are exceptionally strong. O_E operates through gravitational work and dynamical friction. O_I is severely attenuated — this is the "darkness" itself. The system's most dramatic output (cosmic structure) is relational, not informational.

### Element 3: PROCESSING (P) — How Dark Matter Transforms

Multiple distinct processing pathways were identified.

**P_E (Energetic Processing):** Dark matter converts gravitational potential energy to kinetic energy during halo collapse and virialization. The virial theorem governs this conversion: a collapsing halo reaches equilibrium when 2K + U = 0 (twice kinetic energy plus potential energy equals zero). This is a well-defined energetic processing function. In SIDM models, a second processing pathway exists: SIDM allows dark matter particles to collide and exchange energy. This collisional energy exchange represents a distinct thermalization processing channel.

**P_I (Informational Processing):** Severely reduced. In standard CDM, dark matter particles are collisionless and exchange no information beyond gravitational interaction. There is no pattern recognition, no signal processing, no computation in any conventional sense. However, gravitational N-body dynamics does perform a form of processing: out of this seeming chaos emerges remarkable regularity. Simulated dark matter halos have fairly generic radial profiles. The emergence of the universal NFW profile from arbitrary initial conditions suggests that gravitational dynamics performs a form of informational processing — it is an attractor that converts diverse initial conditions into a universal output.

**P_R (Relational Processing):** Dark matter performs extensive relational restructuring. In standard cosmological pictures, dark matter halos are considered to be formed in a hierarchical way; smaller halos first formed from initial density fluctuations and they merged with each other to become larger halos. This hierarchical assembly is a continuous process of relational restructuring: merging substructure, stripping satellites, building filaments, carving voids. The relational processing is rich, multi-scale, and ongoing.

**P_T (Temporal Processing):** Dark matter transforms temporal patterns through dynamical timescale processing. The free-fall time, dynamical time, and relaxation time of a halo are all temporal processing outputs. Phase-space mixing converts initial temporal structure into equilibrium configurations over multiple dynamical times. In SIDM, halos can undergo gravothermal collapse, representing a dramatic temporal processing event: a runaway thermodynamic instability that transforms the halo's temporal evolution from quasi-static to catastrophic.

**Processing Subsystem Assessment:** At least three distinct processing pathways identified: (1) gravitational virialization, (2) hierarchical merger dynamics, and (3) possible SIDM thermalization. P_I is deficient in conventional terms but exhibits emergent informational processing through the gravitational attractor (NFW universality). P_R is exceptionally strong.

### Element 4: CONTROLS (C) — What Constrains Dark Matter

**C_E (Energetic Controls):** Energy conservation governs all dark matter dynamics. The virial theorem acts as a control on halo energetics. Mass-energy conservation constrains the total mass budget. The Jeans mass provides a scale-dependent control on which structures can collapse.

**C_I (Informational Controls):** The Boltzmann equation (or Vlasov equation for collisionless systems) governs the phase-space evolution of dark matter. Liouville's theorem (conservation of phase-space density) provides a fundamental informational control. The NFW profile has a fixed shape, albeit with two scale parameters. The universality of the profile itself functions as an informational control — it constrains the space of possible outcomes.

**C_R (Relational Controls):** Symmetry constraints play a major role. The cosmological principle (homogeneity and isotropy on large scales) constrains the relational structure. Conservation laws (angular momentum, linear momentum) constrain merger outcomes. Any possible fifth force acting on dark matter must be less than 7% the strength of gravity. This observational constraint itself functions as a relational control — it limits the coupling channels available.

**C_T (Temporal Controls):** Causality constrains the light cone and therefore the region of gravitational influence. The Hubble time provides an upper bound on halo ages. The expansion rate H(t) controls the rate of structure formation, acting as a cosmic clock that governs when structures can collapse.

**Controls Assessment:** All four modes operational. The control structure is dominated by fundamental physical laws (conservation laws, causality, Liouville's theorem) rather than engineered or biological rules. This is characteristic of physical systems at the fundamental level, similar to the neutrino case study in the reference file.

### Element 5: FEEDBACK (F) — How Dark Matter Self-Regulates

This element exhibits the most distinctive mode profile of any element.

**F_E (Energetic Feedback):** Energy balance feedback operates through the virial theorem. If a halo acquires mass, it adjusts its velocity dispersion to maintain virial equilibrium. This is a passive but effective energetic feedback loop. In SIDM, active energetic feedback occurs through heat conduction from the outer halo to the inner core.

**F_I (Informational Feedback — Active):** This is perhaps the most important finding. In standard CDM, dark matter has essentially *no active informational feedback*. There are no explicit correction signals, no error-sensing mechanisms, no adaptive responses based on detected deviations. The system operates almost entirely without active feedback in the informational mode. This is a profound structural feature, not a deficiency — it is what makes dark matter collisionless and "dark." The only active informational feedback arises when dark matter is coupled to baryonic matter, where baryonic feedback (supernovae, AGN jets) can reshape the dark matter distribution.

**F_R (Relational Feedback):** Structural integrity is maintained passively through gravitational binding. A halo that satisfies the virial condition persists; one that does not either collapses further or disperses. Tidal interactions provide a form of relational feedback: subhalos that venture too close to the host center are tidally stripped, which is a relational correction mechanism.

**F_T (Temporal Feedback — Passive):** This is the dominant feedback mode. Dark matter halos that persist confirm their own viability by the fact of their continued existence. Over a broad range of halo mass and redshift, the NFW profile approximates the equilibrium configuration of dark matter halos. The remarkable longevity of dark matter halos — persisting over cosmological timescales without dissipation — is the ultimate expression of passive temporal feedback. The system's existence over billions of years *is* the feedback signal.

**Feedback Assessment:** The F_I (active feedback) deficiency is the most consequential mode deficiency in the entire system. It is the root cause of the "nightmare scenario" described by physicists: dark matter interacts with itself and normal matter only through the gravitational force and nothing else. Without active informational feedback, the system cannot be detected through any signal it actively generates. F_T (passive feedback) is maximally strong — dark matter is one of the most persistent structures in the known universe.

### Element 6: INTERFACE (N) — How Dark Matter Mediates Boundaries

This element reveals particularly interesting structure. Multiple distinct interface mechanisms were identified.

**N_E (Energetic Interface):** The gravitational potential well serves as the primary energetic interface. Energy crosses the halo boundary through gravitational infall. Dynamical friction provides a second energetic interface mechanism — massive objects moving through the halo exchange kinetic energy with the dark matter medium. In SIDM models, the self-interaction cross-section itself acts as an energetic interface, mediating energy exchange between particles.

**N_I (Informational Interface):** Gravitational lensing is the primary informational interface between dark matter and the baryonic world. Dark matter appears not to interact via the electromagnetic force, and therefore neither emits nor reflects light. However, it definitely does interact via gravity. Lensing translates the mass distribution (dark matter's internal state) into observable distortions of background light (baryonic information). This is a one-way informational interface: dark matter's mass distribution is readable by baryonic observers, but dark matter receives no electromagnetic information in return. This asymmetry is diagnostically significant.

A second informational interface exists through dynamical effects on baryonic tracers: galaxy rotation curves, velocity dispersions of galaxy clusters, and stellar stream perturbations all translate dark matter's gravitational influence into baryonically observable signals. When galaxies heavy with dark matter cross those streams, astrophysicists can study the disturbances left behind to infer important properties like their speed and density.

**N_R (Relational Interface):** The halo-cosmic web boundary provides a relational interface. Matter flows along filaments into halos, establishing a relational connection between the large-scale structure and individual halo systems. The virial radius serves as the conventional (though somewhat arbitrary) relational boundary. The halo mass function translates cosmological parameters into a distribution of structures, functioning as a relational interface between cosmological context and individual systems.

**N_T (Temporal Interface):** The lookback time / redshift relationship provides a temporal interface between different epochs. Because light travel time allows us to observe halos at different stages of evolution, the temporal interface between "dark matter then" and "observations now" is mediated by the expansion of the universe. The decoherence of primordial perturbations over time provides a temporal interface between initial conditions and present-day structure.

**Interface Assessment:** Multiple distinct interface subsystems identified. N_I is the most analytically rich, with at least three distinct mechanisms (gravitational lensing, rotation curves, stellar stream perturbation). The interface is markedly *asymmetric* — dark matter's state is readable by baryonic systems, but dark matter is informationally "blind" to baryonic signals. This asymmetry has no clear parallel in the reference file's case studies.

**E_I (Informational Environment):** The informational environment for dark matter is impoverished by design. Because dark matter does not couple electromagnetically, the rich photon field that constitutes the informational environment for baryonic systems is invisible to dark matter. The only informational environment is the gravitational field of all other matter.

**E_R (Relational Environment):** Dark matter serves as the scaffolding of the cosmic web, providing the gravitational framework that shapes and holds together galaxies and galaxy clusters. This creates a recursive condition: the relational environment of any given dark matter halo is primarily composed of *other dark matter*. The cosmic web, the filamentary structure connecting halos, the void structure — all are predominantly dark matter. Dark matter is its own relational environment.

**E_T (Temporal Environment):** The expansion rate H(t) and the age of the universe provide the temporal context. The epoch of matter-radiation equality, the epoch of recombination, and the onset of dark energy domination are all temporal environmental features that profoundly shape dark matter's behavior. The Cosmic Web's development follows a clear temporal progression, with early seeds forming at z>100 with initial density fluctuations from quantum fluctuations in the early Universe.

**Environment Assessment:** The dual-role condition (dark matter as both system and environment) is a distinctive finding. E_I is deficient for the same fundamental reason as I_I and O_I: electromagnetic decoupling. E_R exhibits a recursive self-referential structure not seen in the reference file's case studies.

## PART V: IMPLICATIONS FOR DETECTION AND THE CURRENT OBSERVATIONAL LANDSCAPE

The 7ES mode deficiency analysis provides a structural explanation for why dark matter detection has been so difficult: the system's I-mode is deficient across all elements simultaneously. Every detection strategy is, in framework terms, an attempt to exploit the few remaining informational interface channels (N_I):

Gravitational lensing exploits the N_I interface (mass distribution → light distortion). The LSST's highest-priority cosmological mission is weak gravitational lensing: the subtle distortion of background galaxy shapes caused by the gravitational influence of foreground matter, including the invisible dark matter halos that structure the universe.

Rotation curve analysis exploits the N_I interface (mass distribution → orbital velocity).

Direct detection experiments attempt to exploit a hypothetical I_I channel (dark matter particle → detector nucleus scattering) that may not exist.

Collider experiments attempt to *create* dark matter particles, bypassing the I-mode deficiency entirely by manufacturing the system rather than detecting it.

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory, which has begun a 10-year survey to create a new inventory of our solar system and the Milky Way, and chip away at the mystery of dark matter, represents the most ambitious exploitation of the N_I gravitational lensing interface to date. Its 3,200-megapixel camera will photograph the entire southern sky every three nights for 10 years, generating more data than all prior observatories combined.

Recent research on SIDM models suggests that dark matter's mode profile may be richer than the minimal CDM picture. Gravitational lensing observations of three massive galaxy clusters reveal discrepancies with the standard cold dark matter model, particularly in the dense inner regions where sub-halos are more concentrated and abundant than predicted. If SIDM is confirmed, it would add a self-interaction channel that partially remedies the I-mode deficiency by enabling energy and momentum exchange between dark matter particles — effectively upgrading F_E from purely passive to partially active.
