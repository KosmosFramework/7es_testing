### Element 1: INPUT (I) — How Energy, Information, Structure, and Timing Enter the Photon System

The Input element describes how the photon comes into existence — how it is created and launched into the system. This element exhibits clear multi-pathway structure.

**I_E — Energetic Input.** Energy enters the photon system at the moment of emission. The photon's energy is set by E = hν, directly encoding the energy of the transition or process that created it. *Multi-pathway finding:* The energetic input pathway is not singular. Photons can be created through at least six physically distinct mechanisms, each representing a different energetic input subsystem: (a) Atomic/molecular spontaneous emission, where a discrete energy level transition produces a photon of definite frequency. (b) Stimulated emission, where an existing photon field triggers coherent emission, coupling the input energy to the existing field. (c) Bremsstrahlung (braking radiation), where a charged particle decelerating in a Coulomb field produces a continuous-spectrum photon. (d) Synchrotron/cyclotron radiation, where a charged particle in a magnetic field emits due to centripetal acceleration. (e) Pair annihilation (e⁺e⁻ → γγ), where the rest mass of matter–antimatter is converted entirely to photon energy. (f) Thermal/blackbody radiation, where thermal equilibrium processes produce photons following the Planck distribution.

Each of these mechanisms has different energy spectra, different selection rules, and different dependence on environmental conditions. They are parallel subsystems within the Energetic Input mode.

**I_I — Informational Input.** Information enters the photon at creation in the form of its quantum state specification: frequency, polarization, phase, and propagation direction. *Parallel channels:* Frequency and polarization constitute independent informational input channels. **I_R — Relational Input.** The relational context entering the photon system includes the U(1) gauge symmetry of electromagnetism (which constrains the photon to be massless and chargeless), the coupling constant α ≈ 1/137 (fine structure constant), and the selection rules governing which transitions are permitted. **I_T — Temporal Input.** The emission time t₀ sets the phase reference for the photon's wave function. For coherent sources (lasers), the temporal input includes the coherence time of the source. ### Element 2: OUTPUT (O) — How Energy, Information, Structure, and Timing Exit the Photon System

The Output element describes how the photon delivers its contents to the environment — primarily through absorption or scattering. This is the element with the most pronounced multi-pathway structure.

**O_E — Energetic Output.** Energy exits the photon system when it is absorbed or scattered. This is where the most striking multi-pathway structure emerges.

*Three distinct energetic output mechanisms:* (a) **Photoelectric absorption** (dominant at low energies, below ~100 keV in typical materials): the photon is completely absorbed by a bound electron, transferring all its energy. The photon ceases to exist; the electron is ejected. (b) **Compton scattering** (dominant at intermediate energies, ~100 keV to ~10 MeV): the photon transfers partial energy to a free or loosely bound electron. The photon persists but with reduced energy and altered direction. This is a partial output — the photon system continues with modified state. (c) **Pair production** (dominant above ~1.022 MeV, requiring E > 2m_e c²): the photon converts entirely into an electron-positron pair in the presence of a nucleus. Energy is output as rest mass plus kinetic energy of the created particles.

These three pathways are not variations of a single mechanism. They involve different physics (bound-state quantum mechanics, relativistic kinematics, and quantum field creation respectively), different energy thresholds, different products, and different cross-section dependencies on photon energy and target material. **O_I — Informational Output.** The photon delivers information at detection: its frequency (energy measurement), polarization state (polarimetry), arrival direction (imaging), and arrival statistics (photon counting). Each of these is an independent informational output channel.

*Parallel output channels:* In astronomical observation, a single photon detection event simultaneously outputs information about the source's energy (spectroscopy), magnetic field orientation (polarimetry), position (imaging), and temporal behavior (timing). **O_R — Relational Output.** The photon modifies environmental relational structure through its interactions: it can ionize atoms (breaking chemical bonds, altering electronic structure), transfer momentum (radiation pressure, altering spatial relationships), and mediate forces between charges (virtual photon exchange in QED, establishing and modifying electromagnetic relationships between charged particles).

**O_T — Temporal Output.** The detection time t₁ completes the transit-time measurement. For periodic sources, the photon stream carries temporal output as a modulated signal. The photon travels along null geodesics where the proper time interval is dτ = 0. This means that, in the photon's own reference frame (to the extent one can be defined), no time elapses between emission and absorption, regardless of the coordinate distance or time separating these events.

This creates a distinctive situation for Temporal modes throughout the 7ES analysis:

**F_T (Passive Temporal Feedback):** The photon's infinite lifetime and absolute stability represent maximal temporal feedback — it persists indefinitely. **P_T (Temporal Processing):** Phase accumulation φ = ωt occurs in the observer's frame, not in the photon's frame. The photon "processes" time as measured by external clocks, not by any internal clock.

**I_T and O_T (Temporal Input/Output):** Emission and detection times are defined in external reference frames. The photon carries no internal timestamp.

The framework's Temporal mode is implicitly defined from an observer/environment perspective, which is appropriate for the photon. The photon case makes explicit what is implicit in other systems: Temporal modes describe how the system participates in temporal structures, not necessarily how the system internally experiences time. This is a useful clarification for applying the framework to relativistic systems.
