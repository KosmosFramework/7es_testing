**O_I (Informational Output):** Spacetime communicates information to embedded systems through several channels. **O_R (Relational Output):** Spacetime modifies relational structure by forming event horizons (creating causally disconnected regions), by generating causal structure (the light cone at each event determines which events can influence which others), and in extreme cases by changing topology (though classical general relativity is ambiguous on whether topology change actually occurs, and quantum gravity may be needed to resolve this).

**O_T (Temporal Output):** Spacetime's most distinctive temporal output is gravitational time dilation: clocks run at different rates depending on their position in a gravitational field, and spacetime actively determines the rate at which proper time passes for any given observer. Cosmological redshift represents a temporal output at cosmic scales, stretching the wavelength (and thus the effective temporal period) of radiation propagating through expanding spacetime.

**Subsystem Assessment for Output:** Multiple subsystems confirmed, with Energetic and Informational modes showing the clearest multiplicity. The Unruh effect provides yet a third energetic interface: an accelerating observer perceives the vacuum as a thermal bath, converting vacuum energy into perceived thermal energy. These three mechanisms — Hawking radiation at black hole horizons, Gibbons-Hawking radiation at cosmological horizons, and the Unruh effect at acceleration horizons — are physically distinct energetic interfaces with different origins, different temperatures, and different observer-dependence properties.

**N_I (Informational Interface):** The holographic principle, most precisely formulated in the AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena, 1997), establishes that the information content of a volume of spacetime is encoded on its boundary — a profound informational interface that translates between bulk gravitational degrees of freedom and boundary conformal field theory degrees of freedom. **N_R (Relational Interface):** Coordinate transformations between reference frames serve as relational interfaces — the Lorentz transformation translates spatial and temporal relationships between inertial frames, while general coordinate transformations perform this translation in arbitrary frames. The equivalence principle establishes a local relational interface between gravitational and inertial frames: at any point, one can choose coordinates in which the gravitational field vanishes, mapping between the "gravitational" and "inertial" relational structures. The holographic duality (AdS/CFT) simultaneously serves as a relational interface, mapping the geometric relationships of the bulk (distances, curvatures) to the correlation structure of the boundary theory.

**N_T (Temporal Interface):** Gravitational time dilation is a temporal interface in the precise sense of the framework: it synchronizes (or rather, systematically desynchronizes) clocks at different positions in a gravitational field, translating between proper time at different locations. The redshift at event horizons represents an extreme temporal interface where finite proper time for an infalling observer maps to infinite coordinate time for a distant observer. In cosmology, the relationship between conformal time and cosmic time provides a temporal interface that translates between the natural time coordinates for light propagation and for matter dynamics.
