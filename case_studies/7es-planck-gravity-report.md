### Element 1: INPUT — Resources, Signals, Energy, and Information Entering the System

Planck-scale quantum gravity receives inputs through at least four distinguishable subsystems.

**Subsystem 1A — Gravitational Field Inputs.** The classical gravitational field, described by the metric tensor (gμν), connection coefficients, and curvature tensors, enters the quantum gravity regime as the primary structural input. At the Planck scale, these classical field quantities undergo quantization: in Loop Quantum Gravity (LQG), they become holonomies of the Ashtekar-Barbero connection and flux operators; in string theory, they decompose into graviton mode excitations of the string spectrum; in canonical quantum gravity, they become operator-valued distributions acting on a Hilbert space of spatial geometries. The input is the gravitational field itself, but the mechanism by which it enters the quantum regime differs across theoretical programs, establishing Subsystem 1A as internally composite.

**Subsystem 1B — Matter-Energy Inputs.** The energy-momentum tensor (Tμν) of all matter and radiation fields serves as a source term for gravitational dynamics via Einstein's field equations. At Planck-scale energy densities (approximately 5.16 × 10⁹³ kg/m³), matter-energy inputs become extreme enough to probe spacetime microstructure. These inputs include quantum fields in all sectors of the Standard Model, plus any beyond-Standard-Model fields that couple gravitationally. In string theory, these inputs are unified with gravitational inputs at the string scale; in LQG, they are coupled to the gravitational sector through matter Hamiltonians defined on spin-network states. The distinction between this subsystem and 1A reflects the general-relativistic insight that matter-energy and geometry are coupled but categorically distinct input types: geometry tells matter how to move, and matter tells geometry how to curve.

**Subsystem 1C — Quantum Information Inputs.** Developments over the past two decades — including the ER=EPR conjecture (Maldacena and Susskind, 2013), quantum error correction models of spacetime (Almheiri, Dong, and Harlow, 2015), and tensor network constructions of holographic spacetime — indicate that quantum information (entanglement entropy, quantum coherence structure, entanglement patterns) constitutes a fundamental input to spacetime geometry. In this view, the entanglement structure of quantum states is not merely a property carried by matter on a background; it is an input that determines geometric structure itself. This subsystem is mechanistically distinct from 1A and 1B because it operates through information-theoretic rather than field-theoretic channels.

**Subsystem 1D — Topological and Boundary Inputs.** The topology of spatial slices, the specification of boundary conditions (asymptotically flat, asymptotically anti-de Sitter, compact), and the selection of initial/final state boundary data all constitute inputs that constrain the gravitational path integral and determine which quantum gravitational processes are dynamically accessible. In the Hartle-Hawking no-boundary proposal, the choice of boundary condition itself shapes the quantum state of the universe. These inputs are structural and configurational rather than energetic, distinguishing them from subsystems 1A through 1C.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Input is not a single unified function. It operates through at least four mechanistically distinct pathways — geometric, energetic, informational, and topological — each entering the system through different channels and coupling to the processing element through different mechanisms.

### Element 2: OUTPUT — Results, Products, and State Transformations Generated by the System

Planck-scale quantum gravity generates outputs through at least five distinguishable subsystems.

**Subsystem 2A — Emergent Classical Spacetime.** The primary output of quantum gravity is the classical, smooth, continuous spacetime manifold that characterizes gravitational physics at scales much larger than the Planck length. This is the correspondence-limit output: whatever the quantum microstructure of spacetime may be, it must reproduce general relativity and its smooth-manifold geometry in the appropriate classical limit. In LQG, this emergence occurs through the coarse-graining of spin-network states into approximate smooth geometries. In string theory, it emerges through the low-energy effective action of the graviton sector. In Causal Dynamical Triangulations, it arises from the statistical mechanics of simplicial geometries in the continuum limit. The mechanism of emergence differs, but the output itself — classical spacetime — is convergent across approaches.

**Subsystem 2B — Hawking-Bekenstein Thermodynamic Outputs.** Black hole quantum gravity processes produce thermodynamic outputs: Hawking radiation (a thermal spectrum of particles emitted from the event horizon), Bekenstein-Hawking entropy (proportional to horizon area in Planck units, S = A/4ℓ²_P), and — in the final stages of evaporation — a complete transformation of the black hole mass-energy into radiation. These are concrete, calculable outputs that serve as the most robust predictions shared across quantum gravity programs.

**Subsystem 2C — Cosmological Observable Outputs.** Quantum gravitational effects in the very early universe produce outputs observable in cosmological data: modifications to the primordial power spectrum of density perturbations, possible signatures in the cosmic microwave background (tensor-to-scalar ratio, non-Gaussianities), and in Loop Quantum Cosmology, the replacement of the classical Big Bang singularity with a quantum bounce, altering the very early universe's output into subsequent cosmic evolution.

**Subsystem 2D — Quantum Gravitational Corrections to Particle Physics.** At energies approaching the Planck scale, quantum gravity modifies particle physics processes through graviton exchange, modified dispersion relations, minimum-length effects, and (in string theory) the production of string excitations and possible extra-dimensional signatures. These outputs feed into high-energy particle physics as corrections to Standard Model processes.

**Subsystem 2E — Information-Theoretic Outputs.** The resolution of the black hole information paradox requires that quantum gravity unitarily preserves information, producing information-theoretic outputs (the Page curve of entanglement entropy, scrambled but recoverable quantum information in Hawking radiation). This subsystem outputs transformed quantum states rather than classical observables, marking it as mechanistically distinct.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Output operates through multiple channels spanning classical geometry, thermodynamics, cosmological observables, particle physics corrections, and quantum information. These outputs serve different downstream systems and are accessed through different observational and theoretical interfaces.

### Element 3: PROCESSING — Transformation of Inputs into Outputs

Processing exhibits the highest subsystem multiplicity of any element, reflecting the fundamental theoretical open question of quantum gravity. At least five distinct processing pathways have been proposed, each with different mathematical structures and physical assumptions.

**Subsystem 3A — Gravitational Path Integral (Sum Over Geometries).** The most general processing framework integrates over all possible spacetime geometries weighted by the exponential of the Einstein-Hilbert action (or its generalizations), following the Feynman path integral approach extended to gravity. This processing pathway transforms geometric and matter-energy inputs into transition amplitudes between spatial boundary states. The conceptual and mathematical difficulties of defining this integral rigorously (non-renormalizability, conformal factor problem, measure ambiguities) are central to the unsolved status of quantum gravity.

**Subsystem 3B — Spin Foam and Spin Network Processing (Loop Quantum Gravity).** In LQG, processing occurs through the evolution of spin-network states (graphs with edges labeled by SU(2) representations and nodes labeled by intertwiners) via spin-foam amplitudes — discrete, combinatorial structures that represent quantum spacetime histories. The Barrett-Crane and EPRL-FK models specify vertex amplitudes for these spin foams. Processing is fundamentally discrete and combinatorial, operating on a pre-geometric structure from which classical geometry emerges.

**Subsystem 3C — String and Brane Amplitude Processing.** In string/M-theory, processing occurs through worldsheet amplitudes (perturbative string scattering), D-brane dynamics, and non-perturbative dualities (S-duality, T-duality, M-theory unification). The input gravitational degrees of freedom are unified with all other forces as different vibrational modes of fundamental strings or branes in a higher-dimensional spacetime. Processing thus operates in a fundamentally different mathematical structure (worldsheet conformal field theory, higher-dimensional supergravity) than subsystems 3A or 3B.

**Subsystem 3D — Renormalization Group Processing (Asymptotic Safety).** The asymptotic safety program processes quantum gravitational dynamics through the Wetterass-Morris exact renormalization group equation applied to the gravitational effective action. The key processing mechanism is the flow of gravitational coupling constants with energy scale, with the hypothesis that the flow approaches a non-Gaussian ultraviolet fixed point at Planck energies, rendering gravity non-perturbatively renormalizable. Processing here is continuous and field-theoretic, in contrast to the discrete processing of subsystem 3B.

**Subsystem 3E — Holographic Processing (AdS/CFT Duality).** Via the Maldacena correspondence, bulk quantum gravitational processing in (d+1)-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime is exactly dual to boundary conformal field theory processing in d dimensions without gravity. This is not merely a calculational tool; it posits that gravitational processing *is* boundary quantum field theory processing, viewed from a different perspective. The Ryu-Takayanagi formula, entanglement wedge reconstruction, and bulk-boundary operator dictionaries constitute the specific mechanisms of this dual processing pathway.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Processing is the element of greatest structural divergence. The five subsystems identified operate through fundamentally different mathematical structures, make different assumptions about the nature of spacetime at the Planck scale (continuous vs. discrete, 4-dimensional vs. higher-dimensional, geometric vs. pre-geometric), and produce convergent outputs in some domains (black hole entropy, semiclassical limit) but divergent predictions in others. This multiplicity is a structural signature of the domain's theoretical immaturity, and the 7ES decomposition precisely localizes the open problem: it is primarily a Processing question.

### Element 4: CONTROLS — Proactive Constraints Guiding System Behavior

Controls in Planck-scale quantum gravity operate through at least four distinguishable subsystems, each constraining the system's dynamics through a different mechanism.

**Subsystem 4A — Fundamental Symmetry Controls.** Diffeomorphism invariance (general covariance), local Lorentz invariance, and gauge symmetries of matter fields serve as proactive structural constraints embedded in the formulation of any quantum gravity theory. These symmetries dictate which physical configurations are gauge-equivalent (and therefore identical), constrain the form of the action, and generate constraint equations (the Hamiltonian constraint, diffeomorphism constraint, and Gauss constraint in canonical quantum gravity). These are architectural controls — they shape the allowed state space before any dynamics occur.

**Subsystem 4B — Planck-Scale Bounds and Resolution Limits.** The Planck length (ℓ_P ≈ 1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m), Planck time (t_P ≈ 5.391 × 10⁻⁴⁴ s), and Planck energy (E_P ≈ 1.221 × 10¹⁹ GeV) function as fundamental resolution constraints. The generalized uncertainty principle (GUP), ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2[1 + β(Δp)²], modifies the Heisenberg uncertainty relation to impose a minimum measurable length of order ℓ_P. These bounds constrain what states are physically realizable and what measurements are operationally meaningful, functioning as built-in limits on system resolution.

**Subsystem 4C — Conservation and Unitarity Controls.** Energy-momentum conservation (via Noether's theorem and the contracted Bianchi identity), charge conservation, and unitarity (conservation of total probability / information) serve as dynamical constraints that any quantum gravity process must satisfy. Unitarity, in particular, has driven major theoretical developments: the requirement that black hole evaporation preserve information has constrained the space of viable quantum gravity theories and motivated the holographic principle, the firewall debate, and island formula calculations.

**Subsystem 4D — Consistency and Anomaly Controls.** Internal mathematical consistency requirements function as controls at the level of theoretical architecture. In string theory, anomaly cancellation restricts the allowed gauge groups (SO(32) and E₈ × E₈ in heterotic string theory) and the spacetime dimension (10 for superstrings, 11 for M-theory, 26 for bosonic strings). In LQG, the closure constraint on spin-network nodes and the requirement of cylindrical consistency for the kinematic Hilbert space serve analogous roles. These controls are proactive in the sense defined by 7ES_REF_v1.3.txt: they are embedded in the system's structural design and constrain behavior before any execution occurs.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Controls operate across symmetry, scale, conservation, and consistency dimensions. These four subsystems constrain the system through different mechanisms (geometric symmetry, dimensional bounds, dynamical conservation, mathematical consistency) and at different levels of the theoretical architecture.

### Element 5: FEEDBACK — Existential and Operational State Confirmation

The 7ES_REF_v1.3.txt definition of Feedback — encompassing both Active (Dynamic) and Passive (Implicit) modes — proves exceptionally well-suited to Planck-scale quantum gravity. At least three feedback subsystems are identifiable.

**Subsystem 5A — Active Feedback: Semiclassical Backreaction.** The semiclassical Einstein equation, Gμν = 8πG⟨Tμν⟩, describes how the quantum expectation value of the matter stress-energy tensor modifies the classical spacetime geometry, which in turn alters the quantum field dynamics, which modifies the stress-energy expectation value. This is an explicit dynamic feedback loop in the classical sense: the output of quantum field processing on curved spacetime feeds back as a corrective input to the geometric structure. This feedback loop drives phenomena such as black hole evaporation (Hawking radiation back-reacts on the black hole geometry, causing mass loss) and cosmological particle creation (quantum fluctuations during inflation become density perturbations that modify the subsequent expansion history).

**Subsystem 5B — Active Feedback: Holographic Information Feedback.** The holographic principle, and specifically the covariant entropy bound (Bousso bound), establishes that the maximum information content of a spacetime region is constrained by its boundary area in Planck units. This functions as active feedback because it is a dynamical constraint that responds to the system's state: as a region's information content approaches the bound, the system's behavior is modified (most dramatically, through gravitational collapse to a black hole). The Ryu-Takayanagi formula and its quantum extensions provide specific mechanisms by which boundary entanglement entropy feeds back into bulk geometric structure.

**Subsystem 5C — Passive Feedback: Structural Persistence of Spacetime.** This subsystem directly invokes the 7ES_REF_v1.3.txt definition of passive feedback: "the mere persistence of the system's structure and function, which serves as a continuous confirmation that its processes are within viable parameters." The stable existence of spacetime structure — whether described as a smooth manifold, a spin network, a string vacuum, or a simplicial complex — constitutes passive feedback that the quantum gravitational system remains coherent. The persistence of the current vacuum state (with its particular values of coupling constants, particle masses, and cosmological constant) is continuous implicit feedback that the system's internal conditions remain within the domain of viability. This is directly analogous to the reference file's examples of proton stability and crystal binding as passive feedback, extended to the most fundamental structural level: spacetime itself.

This subsystem has particular analytical power at the Planck scale because it resolves the problem of identifying feedback in systems where no explicit cybernetic signaling loop exists. A stable spin-network state in LQG, a consistent string vacuum, or a non-singular bouncing cosmology in LQC does not "send a signal" confirming its coherence — its continued existence *is* the confirmation. The 7ES passive feedback concept thus fills a genuine analytical gap that purely cybernetic frameworks cannot address in fundamental physics.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Feedback operates in both active and passive modes, with active feedback further subdivided into semiclassical backreaction and holographic information channels. The passive feedback mode proves indispensable for applying the framework to fundamental physics, validating the 7ES_REF_v1.3.txt refinement of the classical cybernetic concept.

### Element 6: INTERFACE — Boundaries and Interaction Modalities

Interface structure in Planck-scale quantum gravity is exceptionally rich, with at least four distinguishable subsystems mediating different types of boundary interactions.

**Subsystem 6A — The Planck-Scale Quantum-Classical Interface.** The Planck scale itself constitutes the primary interface between the quantum gravitational regime and the classical gravitational regime. Below approximately ℓ_P, spacetime is expected to exhibit quantum discreteness, quantum superposition of geometries, and/or stringy structure; above it, the familiar smooth-manifold classical geometry of general relativity applies. This interface is not sharp (it likely involves a gradual transition or crossover), and its precise character is one of the central questions of quantum gravity. In LQG, the interface is mediated by coherent states that approximate classical geometries; in string theory, by the low-energy effective action; in asymptotic safety, by the crossover between the UV fixed point and classical scaling regimes.

**Subsystem 6B — Black Hole Horizon Interface.** The event horizon of a black hole functions as an interface where quantum gravitational effects become physically manifest. The stretched horizon concept (Susskind, Thorlacius, and Uglum, 1993) explicitly treats the near-horizon region as a membrane-like interface with physical properties (temperature, entropy, viscosity, electrical conductivity). The firewall debate (Almheiri, Marolf, Polchinski, and Sully, 2012) centers on the physical character of this interface: is it smooth (as general relativity predicts), or does quantum gravity modify it into a high-energy boundary? This interface mediates the exchange of energy (Hawking radiation), information (Page curve), and geometric structure between the black hole interior and exterior.

**Subsystem 6C — Holographic (AdS/CFT) Boundary Interface.** In the AdS/CFT correspondence, the conformal boundary of anti-de Sitter spacetime serves as a holographic interface between bulk quantum gravity and boundary quantum field theory. This is perhaps the most precisely characterized interface in quantum gravity: the bulk-boundary operator dictionary, the HKLL (Hamilton-Kabat-Lifschytz-Lowe) reconstruction procedure, and entanglement wedge reconstruction specify exactly how information crosses this interface. This subsystem is mechanistically unique because it mediates an exchange not between spatial regions of the same theory, but between two different physical descriptions (gravitational and non-gravitational) of the same system.

**Subsystem 6D — Cosmological Horizon and Phase-Transition Interfaces.** The cosmological horizon in de Sitter space, the interface between pre-geometric and geometric phases of spacetime (relevant to the very early universe and to condensate models of emergent gravity), and the boundaries between topologically distinct spacetime sectors all function as interfaces mediating quantum gravitational interactions. In Loop Quantum Cosmology, the bounce surface — the moment of maximum density where the contracting universe transitions to expansion — serves as a temporal interface across which the quantum gravitational state must evolve coherently.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Interface is a multi-scale, multi-type element in this domain, encompassing scale-transition interfaces (6A), causal-boundary interfaces (6B), duality-boundary interfaces (6C), and phase/cosmological interfaces (6D). The diversity of interface types reflects the richness of boundary structure in quantum gravitational systems.

### Element 7: ENVIRONMENT — External Conditions and Contexts

The environment of Planck-scale quantum gravity comprises at least four distinguishable external context systems.

**Subsystem 7A — Classical Spacetime Environment.** The larger-scale classical spacetime in which Planck-scale processes are embedded provides the gravitational and geometric context. The background curvature, global topology, and asymptotic structure of the ambient spacetime all influence Planck-scale dynamics. For Planck-scale processes near a black hole, the environment includes the external Schwarzschild or Kerr geometry; for early-universe quantum gravity, the environment includes the cosmological expansion history.

**Subsystem 7B — Quantum Vacuum Environment.** The quantum vacuum state — with its virtual particle fluctuations, vacuum energy density, and Casimir-type effects — constitutes the quantum field-theoretic environment of Planck-scale gravity. The cosmological constant problem (the discrepancy between predicted and observed vacuum energy) directly involves the relationship between Planck-scale quantum gravity and its vacuum environment. Zero-point fluctuations of all quantum fields contribute to the gravitational dynamics at the Planck scale.

**Subsystem 7C — Cosmological and Astrophysical Environment.** The large-scale cosmological context — including the expansion rate (Hubble parameter), matter-energy content (matter density, radiation density, dark energy density), and the cosmic microwave background — provides the observational environment against which quantum gravitational predictions are tested. This environment also includes the astrophysical context of black holes, neutron stars, and gravitational wave sources where quantum gravity effects might become detectable.

**Subsystem 7D — Theoretical Landscape Environment.** In string theory, the landscape of possible vacuum states (estimated at 10⁵⁰⁰ or more) constitutes a meta-environment: the space of all possible low-energy effective theories consistent with the fundamental string dynamics. The selection of the particular vacuum corresponding to our universe (with its specific particle physics and cosmological constant) is an environment-level question. More broadly, the space of all consistent quantum gravity theories — constrained by the swampland conjectures — forms a theoretical environment within which any specific quantum gravity model operates.

**Subsystem Assessment:** Environment spans classical, quantum, cosmological, and theoretical-landscape scales, each providing different types of external context, constraints, and opportunities for the quantum gravitational system.
