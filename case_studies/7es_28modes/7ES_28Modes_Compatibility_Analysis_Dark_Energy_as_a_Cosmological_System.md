## Part II: Element-by-Element Analysis Across Four Modes

### Element 1: INPUT (I) — Four Modes

**I_E — Energetic Input:**
Dark energy constitutes approximately 69.4% of the total energy density of the universe. The energetic input to the dark energy system depends critically on which theoretical model is operative. Under the cosmological constant model (Λ), there is no external energetic input in the conventional sense — the energy density is a fixed property of spacetime itself, approximately 5.95 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³. As space expands, for the energy density to remain constant in a given volume, more dark energy must come into being. This represents a unique I_E pathway: energy input is not from an external reservoir crossing a boundary, but from the intrinsic expansion of the substrate. Under quintessence models, quintessence is a dynamic, time-evolving and spatially dependent form of energy with negative pressure sufficient to drive accelerating expansion, meaning I_E involves the scalar field's kinetic and potential energy, with energy input governed by the field's rolling down its potential V(φ).

This element exhibits **multiple distinct subsystems**: the Λ-pathway (constant energy density from vacuum) and the quintessence-pathway (dynamical energy from a scalar field potential) are fundamentally different I_E mechanisms. A third pathway emerges from modified gravity models, where the "input" is a geometric modification to spacetime curvature rather than a conventional energy source.

**I_I — Informational Input:**
Dark energy receives essentially no informational input in the conventional sense — it does not absorb signals, process data, or respond to external information. However, under interacting dark energy models, the interaction between dark matter and dark energy can be incorporated into field theory models that have proved successful in alleviating the coincidence problem. In such models, the dark matter density profile constitutes an informational input that modulates dark energy's behavior. Under quintessence, the scalar field φ responds to the local matter density through gravitational coupling, constituting a minimal but real I_I channel. The MSW-like effect in the neutrino case study from the reference file provides a useful analog: just as matter density modifies neutrino oscillation, matter density may modulate dark energy's equation of state in interacting models.

**I_R — Relational Input:**
The relational input to dark energy consists of the spacetime metric g_μν and the cosmological boundary conditions. The Friedmann Equations describe how the universe expands or contracts over time given its contents: matter, radiation, and dark energy, emerging from Einstein's General Relativity when applied to a homogeneous and isotropic universe. The FRW metric symmetry constraints (homogeneity, isotropy) are relational inputs that constrain how dark energy can manifest. The spatial curvature parameter k and the Hubble parameter H(t) serve as relational inputs defining the geometric context within which dark energy operates.

**I_T — Temporal Input:**
The cosmic time coordinate and the scale factor a(t) serve as temporal inputs. DESI's baryon acoustic oscillation results, combined with Type Ia supernovae and CMB data, showed a 2.5- to 3.9-sigma preference for time-varying dark energy models over the standard Lambda-CDM cosmology. If dark energy is time-varying, then cosmic epoch itself becomes a critical temporal input determining the current equation of state. The age of the universe and the redshift at which dark energy began dominating (z ≈ 0.4, approximately 7–8 billion years ago) constitute temporal boundary conditions.

### Element 2: OUTPUT (O) — Four Modes

This O_E channel is the most observationally significant output of the system. Negative pressure induces a repulsive term in Newton's law which dominates on large scales, with a critical scale beyond which antigravity dominates the dynamics of approximately 1 Mpc.

Multiple O_E subsystems are evident: (1) the metric expansion acceleration, (2) the dilution of matter density (by increasing volume while dark energy density remains constant or nearly so), and (3) at late cosmic times, the potential future effects such as the "Big Rip" scenario if w < −1 (phantom energy).

**O_I — Informational Output:**

All information about dark energy is inferred indirectly through its gravitational effects on luminous matter and radiation. This represents a near-total O_I deficiency, which is diagnostically significant: it is the primary reason dark energy remains the most poorly understood component of the universe.

**O_R — Relational Output:**

**O_T — Temporal Output:**

The future de Sitter horizon, if dark energy persists, will impose an eternal exponential expansion rhythm.

### Element 3: PROCESSING (P) — Four Modes

This element exhibits the most pronounced **multiple distinct subsystems** of any element in the analysis.

These three P_E pathways are not merely variations of a single mechanism — they represent fundamentally different physical ontologies (vacuum property vs. dynamical field vs. geometric modification).

Under interacting dark energy models, the coupling to dark matter introduces genuine P_I: a quintessence scalar field can be coupled to dark matter through momentum and energy exchanges, where momentum transfer is weighed by an interaction between the field derivative and dark matter four-velocity.

## Part VI: Implications for the Mode Multiplex Theorem

Dark energy provides a significant test case for the Mode Multiplex Theorem (Theorem 1.1.1). The theorem asserts that each element of a viable system necessarily manifests in at least four modes. Dark energy tests the limits of "necessarily manifests":

The I-mode across most elements is present only in the most minimal sense (e.g., I_I exists only through gravitational coupling, O_I is absent for direct signals). The question is whether these minimal or latent modes satisfy the theorem's requirements. The reference file's Mode Deficiency criterion (Definition 1.5.1) states that a single mode deficiency can cause system failure. Dark energy's I-mode deficiency has not caused the system to fail — dark energy persists and dominates the cosmic energy budget.

This suggests one of two interpretations:

(1) Dark energy's I-mode functions are not truly deficient but merely below our observational threshold. The system has sufficient internal variety in the informational dimension; we simply cannot access it.

(2) Dark energy represents a class of system where the Mode Multiplex Theorem's requirement for all four modes is satisfied at a minimal rather than robust level, and the system compensates through overwhelming variety in E-mode and T-mode.

The second interpretation aligns with the Mode Complementarity Principle (Principle 7.1.1): dark energy may be optimizing E-mode and T-mode at the expense of I-mode and R-mode.
