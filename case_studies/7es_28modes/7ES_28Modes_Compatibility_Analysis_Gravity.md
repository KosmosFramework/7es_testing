**I_E — Energetic Input.** The primary energetic input to the gravitational system is mass-energy in all its forms. Rest mass, kinetic energy, electromagnetic field energy, thermal energy, and even the energy stored in other gravitational fields all contribute to T₀₀ (the energy density component). The universality of gravitational coupling — the fact that gravity responds to *all* forms of energy without exception — means I_E has no selectivity filter. Every form of energy that exists within or crosses the system boundary contributes. This universality is itself a distinguishing feature: unlike electromagnetism (which couples only to charge) or the strong force (which couples only to color charge), gravity's energetic input channel accepts everything. The diversity of energy forms feeding into gravity constitutes multiple parallel energetic input subsystems: mass accretion, radiative energy absorption, pressure contributions (significant in relativistic regimes such as stellar interiors and the early universe), and momentum flux.

**I_I — Informational Input.** The informational input to gravity is the spatial distribution and configuration of mass-energy. It is not sufficient to know *how much* energy is present (I_E); gravity requires information about *where* it is and *how it is moving*. The full T_μν encodes this: T₀ᵢ components carry momentum density information (which direction mass-energy is flowing), while Tᵢⱼ components carry stress and pressure information (how mass-energy is distributed and deforming internally). In the Newtonian limit, this reduces to the mass density field ρ(x,y,z), but the full relativistic treatment requires all 10 components. Gravitational wave detection provides another informational input channel: the strain pattern h_μν encodes information about distant astrophysical events. The LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA detector network demonstrates that gravity carries rich informational content about source masses, spins, orbital parameters, and distances.

**I_R — Relational Input.** The relational inputs to gravity include boundary conditions, spacetime topology, and the connectivity structure of the mass distribution. In solving the Einstein equations, one must specify the topology of the spacetime manifold (is it simply connected? does it have handles or boundaries?), the boundary conditions (asymptotic flatness, cosmological boundary conditions, junction conditions at material interfaces), and the symmetry properties of the source distribution. These are genuinely relational inputs — they specify not *what* is present but *how things are connected*. The choice of spacetime topology is an input that cannot be derived from the field equations themselves; it must be supplied as a relational boundary condition. Additionally, in multi-body gravitational systems, the relative positions and orientations of gravitating objects constitute relational inputs that determine the character of the gravitational interaction.

**I_T — Temporal Input.** Temporal inputs include initial data on a spacelike hypersurface (the Cauchy data: the 3-metric γᵢⱼ and extrinsic curvature Kᵢⱼ at time t₀), time-dependent changes in the source distribution (a collapsing star, merging black holes, an expanding universe), and external timing references such as the cosmic expansion rate H(t). This is a genuine temporal input subsystem — the system cannot process without it.

**Subsystem Assessment for Input:** At least four distinct input subsystems operate through different physical mechanisms. ### Element 2: OUTPUT (O) — How Gravity Acts on Its Environment

Gravity's outputs are the observable effects of spacetime curvature on matter, energy, light, and time. These outputs operate through multiple physically distinct mechanisms.

**O_E — Energetic Output.** Gravitational systems output energy through several channels. Gravitational waves carry energy away from accelerating masses (binary pulsars lose orbital energy at the rate predicted by GR, as confirmed by the Hulse-Taylor pulsar observations). Tidal heating converts gravitational potential energy into thermal energy in deformable bodies (Jupiter's moon Io is heated volcanically by tidal forces). Gravitational potential energy release powers accretion disk luminosity around compact objects, converting gravitational energy to electromagnetic radiation with efficiencies up to approximately 42% for maximally spinning black holes — far exceeding nuclear fusion's roughly 0.7% efficiency. Hawking radiation, if confirmed, would represent another energetic output channel: black holes converting gravitational/vacuum energy into thermal radiation.

**O_I — Informational Output.** Gravity outputs information primarily through geodesic determination — it tells matter and light *how to move*. The geodesic equation is an informational output: given the spacetime metric, it computes trajectories. Gravitational lensing outputs angular position and magnification information about background sources, allowing gravity to function as a cosmic telescope. Gravitational wave signals carry detailed information about their sources (chirp mass, mass ratio, spin, distance, sky location) — the LIGO detections have demonstrated that gravitational wave astronomy is a rich informational output channel. The Shapiro time delay outputs timing information that has been used to test GR to high precision.

**O_R — Relational Output.** Gravity modifies the relational structure of spacetime itself. It creates event horizons (introducing new causal boundaries that partition spacetime into causally disconnected regions), modifies geodesic connectivity (two nearby geodesics may diverge or converge depending on curvature), and alters the causal structure (light cones tilt near massive objects, changing which events can influence which). In extreme cases, gravity can change spacetime topology: the formation of a black hole singularity represents a topological modification of the spacetime manifold. Gravitational structure formation (galaxies, clusters, the cosmic web) represents relational output on cosmological scales — gravity organizes matter into hierarchical structures, creating new relational patterns.

**O_T — Temporal Output.** Gravitational time dilation is a direct temporal output: clocks at different gravitational potentials run at different rates. This is not merely a measurement artifact but a genuine physical effect (GPS satellites must correct for it). Frame-dragging (the Lense-Thirring effect) outputs a temporal signature by causing the precession of gyroscopes and orbital planes near rotating masses. Gravitational wave oscillation frequencies are temporal outputs that encode the dynamics of their source systems. The cosmological redshift is a temporal output of the expanding universe's gravitational dynamics, stretching photon wavelengths and effectively slowing the "clock rate" of distant sources as seen by local observers.

**Subsystem Assessment for Output:** At least five distinct output subsystems were identified: static gravitational fields (geodesic determination), gravitational radiation (wave emission), tidal forces (differential acceleration), frame-dragging (rotational spacetime effects), and gravitational lensing (light deflection and magnification). Each operates through a different physical mechanism and can in principle be present or absent independently (a non-rotating, static mass exhibits static fields and tidal forces but no frame-dragging and no gravitational waves).

**Subsystem Assessment for Processing:** Three primary processing subsystems are clearly distinct. First, the Einstein field equations (10 coupled nonlinear PDEs) process mass-energy into curvature. In neutron stars, this effect is substantial: the gravitational binding energy is approximately 10-20% of the total mass-energy, meaning the star's own gravitational field significantly modifies its gravitational influence. This is explicit, active informational feedback — the system monitors its own output and uses it to modify its processing. In gravitational wave interactions, this nonlinearity manifests as wave-wave scattering: gravitational waves can interact with each other because each wave's energy generates additional curvature. The continued existence of a gravitationally bound structure is itself confirmation of the system's viability, exactly matching the framework's definition of passive feedback. Gravitational wave memory (a permanent displacement of test masses after a gravitational wave passes) is a fascinating temporal feedback effect: the passage of the wave leaves a lasting imprint on the spacetime, a form of "gravitational memory" that persists indefinitely.

**Subsystem Assessment for Feedback:** The active/passive distinction maps cleanly onto gravity. Active feedback (F_I) is instantiated by the nonlinear self-coupling of the field equations.
