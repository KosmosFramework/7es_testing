## Part I: Input (I) — Four Modes

The CMB's input element exhibits clear multiplicity across all four modes, with several distinct physical subsystems operating in parallel.

**I_E — Energetic Input.** The CMB is relic radiation from the hot, dense early universe, produced shortly after the Big Bang, which became transparent to photons approximately 300,000 years after the Big Bang during the decoupling phase. The primary energetic input is the thermal energy of the primordial photon-baryon plasma at recombination, when the universe had cooled to approximately 3000 K. This energy was encoded in the blackbody spectrum of the radiation field at the surface of last scattering. Utilizing experimental data from the COBE FIRAS instrument, the CMB monopole spectrum was measured in the frequency range of 60 GHz to 600 GHz at a temperature of 2.728 K. A second distinct energetic input subsystem operates through the gravitational potential wells that existed at recombination — the Sachs-Wolfe effect, in which photons climbing out of potential wells lost energy and those in underdense regions gained it. A third energetic input channel is the energy injected into the photon field from dissipation of primordial density perturbations via Silk damping. Photons diffusing through the primordial plasma erased perturbations on small scales, and this Silk damping process has been observed in the CMB anisotropies. Thus I_E manifests through at least three distinct physical subsystems: thermal equilibrium radiation, gravitational potential energy, and dissipative energy injection.

**I_I — Informational Input.** The informational input to the CMB encodes at minimum four distinct types of information through independent physical mechanisms. First, the temperature of the plasma at recombination encodes the baryon-to-photon ratio. The peak values of the CMB power spectrum hold important information about the physical properties of the early universe: the first peak determines the overall curvature of the universe, while the second and third peaks detail the density of normal matter and dark matter, respectively. Second, the primordial density perturbations seeded by inflation imprint a nearly scale-invariant power spectrum. The fluctuation modes corresponding to scales greater than one degree capture the initial conditions imprinted at the end of inflation. Third, the polarization pattern at last scattering carries independent informational content about the local quadrupole anisotropy. Fourth, gravitational waves from inflation would imprint a tensor perturbation signal distinguishable from scalar perturbations. These are parallel informational input subsystems, each encoding distinct physical parameters through distinct mechanisms.

**I_R — Relational Input.** The relational input encompasses the structural constraints and contextual relationships that shaped the CMB. These include the baryon-photon coupling relationship (Thomson scattering cross-section), the dark matter gravitational potential structure providing the "wells" for acoustic oscillation, the tight coupling between baryons and photons through which matter tends to collapse due to gravity onto overdense regions, while photons resist this collapse and push the baryons outward, creating oscillatory modes of compression and rarefaction, and the cosmological parameters (Ωb, Ωc, H₀, ΩΛ) that collectively constrain the geometry and dynamics of the system. These are distinct relational subsystems: scattering cross-section constraints, gravitational topology, and geometric boundary conditions.

**I_T — Temporal Input.** The temporal inputs include the age of the universe at recombination (approximately 380,000 years), which sets the sound horizon scale; recombination happened at approximately 380,000 years after the Big Bang corresponding to a redshift of about 1090. The Hubble expansion rate H(t) provides a temporal clock reference. The finite thickness of the last scattering surface (not instantaneous but occurring over a redshift range Δz ≈ 80) provides a temporal input that determines the damping of small-scale anisotropies. Because the proton-electron plasma does not recombine instantaneously, the last scattering surface has a finite thickness, and photon diffusion results in damping at the smallest scales. These represent at least three distinct temporal input subsystems.

## Part II: Output (O) — Four Modes

**O_E — Energetic Output.** The CMB's energetic output is the radiation field itself — a 2.725 K relic blackbody radiation filling the universe, peaking near 160 GHz. Its energy density exceeds that of all the photons emitted by all the stars in the history of the universe. The energetic output operates through multiple channels: the monopole (uniform blackbody spectrum), the dipole (kinematic Doppler effect from our motion through the CMB rest frame), and the higher-order anisotropies representing temperature fluctuations at the level of ΔT/T ≈ 10⁻⁵. Each represents a physically distinct output subsystem.

**O_I — Informational Output.** The informational output is exceptionally rich and multi-channeled. The temperature power spectrum (TT) encodes cosmological parameters through its peak structure. The E-mode polarization power spectrum carries complementary information. E-mode polarization, a curl-free pattern giving polarization vectors that are radial around cold spots and tangential around hot spots, is generated by both density and gravitational wave perturbations. The B-mode polarization pattern, if detected at primordial levels, would carry independent information about gravitational waves. B-mode, a divergence-free polarization mode with vorticity, can only be produced at recombination by gravitational waves. The cross-correlations (TE, TB, EB) constitute additional independent informational output channels. These are distinct output subsystems operating through different physical encoding mechanisms.

**O_R — Relational Output.** The CMB modifies the relational environment by providing the photon bath that participates in inverse Compton scattering with hot gas in galaxy clusters (the thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect), creating new relational structures between the radiation field and matter. The cosmic microwave background radiation is the dominant radiation field in the Universe and one of the most powerful cosmological tools yet found. The CMB also provides the reference frame against which peculiar velocities of structures are measured (kinetic SZ effect), establishing a relational coordinate system for the observable universe.

**O_T — Temporal Output.** The CMB provides a temporal output through its temperature-redshift relationship T(z) = T₀(1+z), serving as a cosmic clock. Knowing the dependence of temperature T on the redshift z allows study of the thermodynamic and radiative state of the Universe many years ago. The acoustic peak spacing serves as a "temporal ruler" encoding the sound horizon at last scattering. The CMB's continuous existence through cosmic time constitutes a persistent temporal output signal.

## Part III: Processing (P) — Four Modes

The Processing element exhibits the most striking multiplicity, with at least four major parallel processing pathways operating through entirely distinct physical mechanisms.

**P_E — Energetic Processing.** Multiple energetic transformation subsystems operate within the CMB. First, the adiabatic cooling of the radiation field via cosmic expansion (T ∝ 1/a) continuously transforms the energy distribution. Second, Silk damping can be understood in terms of mixing of blackbodies creating y-type distortion which comptonizes to i-type and μ-type distortions. Third, photon diffusion through the baryon-photon plasma converts organized acoustic energy into thermal energy. Fourth, the Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect represents energy processing through inverse Compton scattering, where CMB photons gain energy from hot electrons. These are four distinct energetic processing subsystems operating through different physics.

**P_I — Informational Processing.** The informational processing is equally multi-channeled. The acoustic oscillations in the baryon-photon plasma represent the dominant informational processing mechanism — the gas heats as it compresses and cools as it expands, creating fluctuations in the temperature of the CMB, which manifests as a series of peaks in the angular power spectrum. Gravitational lensing by large-scale structure constitutes a second informational processing subsystem, smoothing out the acoustic oscillations observed in the CMB power spectra by several percent, and transforming some polarization E-modes into B-modes. The integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect represents a third processing channel, where CMB photons pick up a net redshift or blueshift as they move through gravitational potentials that are not constant in time. Thomson scattering during reionization constitutes a fourth informational processing pathway, causing uniform damping of CMB anisotropies at scales below the cosmological horizon at the epoch of reionization, while also scattering photons into the line of sight carrying nonzero net polarization.

**P_R — Relational Processing.** Relational processing occurs through the restructuring of photon-baryon coupling at recombination (transition from tightly coupled fluid to free-streaming photons), through gravitational lensing (which remaps angular relationships), and through the conversion between E-mode and B-mode polarization patterns by gravitational lensing and cosmic birefringence. Gravitational lensing turns part of the E-modes into B-modes through the intervening gravitational potential of the large-scale distribution of matter.

**P_T — Temporal Processing.** Temporal processing manifests through acoustic oscillation phase evolution (the "freezing in" of oscillation phases at decoupling), where the temporal phases of the oscillations were frozen-in at recombination and became projected on the sky as a harmonic series of peaks and troughs in power. The cosmological redshifting of the spectrum through expansion represents continuous temporal processing that has operated for 13.8 billion years. The finite duration of recombination itself acts as a temporal processing filter, damping modes whose oscillation period is shorter than the recombination timescale.

**F_T — Temporal Feedback (Passive).** The CMB's continued existence over 13.8 billion years of cosmic time is a textbook example of passive temporal feedback as defined by the framework.
